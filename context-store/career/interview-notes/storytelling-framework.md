# Interview Storytelling: Give the Taste, Not the Recipe

Source: PM coaching insight, May 2026

## The Core Problem

Senior PMs bomb hiring manager rounds by listing every detail of their work — "first we did this, then this, then this and this and this..." — 40+ real, impressive, completely useless details.

The hiring manager didn't need the recipe. They needed the taste.

## The Shift

| What 50 details prove | What one well-chosen example proves |
|---|---|
| You executed | You can lead |
| You know the steps | You know which step mattered |
| You're proud of your work | You understand what they're buying |

Hiring managers have their own problems, their own constraints, their own context. They don't need your steps. They need to see your judgment.

## The Pattern That Works

> "I prioritized automation across the org. The biggest unlock was automating the one task that happened 17 times a day across 50 people. Here's how I made the call, and here's what changed."

That's a taste. That's enough. They fill in the rest.

## How to Use This in Prep

For every story in the bank:
1. Identify the **single most representative moment** — one sentence that captures the decision or insight
2. Lead with that. Expand only if they ask.
3. Closing move: "If you want I can go deeper on how we got there" — hands them control

**Red flag phrase:** "And then... and then... and then..."

**Kill signal:** If you're listing steps chronologically, stop and reframe to the decision or outcome.

## The Diagnostic

If interviews end with "great experience but not the right fit" — this is probably why. You're selling the cookbook instead of the dish.
